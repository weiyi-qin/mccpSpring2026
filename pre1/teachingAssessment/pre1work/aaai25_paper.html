Doubly-Bounded Queue for Constrained Online Learning: Keeping Pace with Dynamics of Both Loss and Constraint

Paper status: Anonymous submission

This HTML page is a cleaned reconstruction of the provided PDF. Broken placeholders, malformed equations, and appendix fragments from the previous version have been removed. Mathematical expressions are rendered in plain text for readability.

Abstract

We consider online convex optimization with time-varying constraints and conduct performance analysis using two stringent metrics: dynamic regret with respect to the online solution benchmark, and hard constraint violation that does not allow any compensated violation over time. We propose an efficient algorithm called Constrained Online Learning with Doubly-bounded Queue (COLDQ), which introduces a novel virtual queue that is both lower and upper bounded, allowing tight control of the constraint violation without the need for the Slater condition. We prove via a new Lyapunov drift analysis that COLDQ achieves O(T^((1 + Vx) / 2)) dynamic regret and O(T^Vg) hard constraint violation, where Vx and Vg capture the dynamics of the loss and constraint functions. For the first time, the two bounds smoothly approach the best-known O(T^(1/2)) regret and O(1) violation, as the dynamics of the losses and constraints diminish. For strongly convex loss functions, COLDQ matches the best-known O(log T) static regret while maintaining the O(T^Vg) hard constraint violation. We further introduce an expert-tracking variation of COLDQ, which achieves the same performance bounds without any prior knowledge of the system dynamics. Simulation results demonstrate that COLDQ outperforms the state-of-the-art approaches.

1. Introduction

In many online learning applications, system states change over time with unknown distributions. Both optimization losses and constraints may depend on these states, and are therefore dynamic. Online Convex Optimization (OCO) is a standard framework for such problems, with applications including advertisement placement, load balancing, network virtualization, and resource allocation.

In standard OCO, the learner selects an online decision from a known convex set, while the current loss function is revealed only after the decision has been made. This motivates regret minimization. Earlier work showed that dynamic regret can smoothly approach the classic O(T^(1/2)) static regret bound as the variation of the loss functions becomes smaller, meaning the online algorithm can keep pace with the dynamics of the losses.

Strict projection onto feasible sets can be computationally expensive. In many practical systems, some temporary constraint violations are acceptable. Prior work therefore studied soft constraint violation, which allows compensation over time. In contrast, hard constraint violation is stricter: it does not allow a violation at one time step to be canceled by satisfaction at another. For fixed constraints, the best-known soft and hard violation bounds are both O(1).

For time-varying constraints, however, prior work did not fully recover this fixed-constraint behavior. Existing bounds can become smaller when the dynamics reduce, but they do not smoothly approach the best-known O(1) hard constraint violation. In that sense, earlier constrained OCO methods did not keep pace with the dynamics of the constraints.

The paper answers the following question in the affirmative: can a constrained OCO algorithm achieve a dynamic regret bound and a hard constraint violation bound that smoothly approach O(T^(1/2)) and O(1), respectively, as the dynamics of both the losses and the constraints diminish?

Main Contributions

  • The paper proposes COLDQ, a constrained online learning algorithm with a doubly-bounded virtual queue. Unlike previous approaches that use either a lower bound or an upper bound on the queue, COLDQ enforces both, and does so without the Slater condition.
  • It develops a new Lyapunov drift analysis that leverages both queue bounds, yielding O(T^((1 + Vx) / 2)) dynamic regret and O(T^Vg) hard constraint violation.
  • For strongly convex losses, COLDQ matches the best-known O(log T) static regret while preserving the O(T^Vg) hard constraint violation guarantee.
  • The paper also gives an expert-tracking extension of COLDQ that removes the need to know the system dynamics in advance while keeping the same asymptotic guarantees.
  • Experiments on time-varying constraints, fixed constraints, and online job scheduling show that COLDQ outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods.

2. Related Work

2.1 OCO with Fixed Constraints

The paper reviews the classic OCO regret results of Zinkevich (2003) and Hazan, Agarwal, and Kale (2007), then discusses how later work relaxed hard per-round feasibility into long-term constraints to reduce the cost of projection. Mahdavi, Jin, and Yang (2012) and Jenatton, Huang, and Archambeau (2016) analyzed soft constraint violation, while Yu and Neely (2020) achieved O(1) violation under the Slater condition. For hard constraint violation, Yuan and Lamperski (2018) and Yi et al. (2021) established stronger guarantees but still under more restrictive settings than the one studied here.

2.2 OCO with Time-Varying Constraints

For stochastic or time-varying constraints, prior work obtained a range of static regret, dynamic regret, and soft violation bounds. The paper highlights that even the best earlier results do not smoothly recover O(1) hard violation as the system becomes less dynamic. In particular, RECOO (Guo et al. 2022) provides strong performance for fixed constraints and O(T^(3/4)) hard violation for time-varying constraints, but still does not fully keep pace with the dynamics of the constraints.

3. Constrained Online Convex Optimization

The constrained OCO problem is modeled as an iterative game over T time slots. At time t, the learner chooses a decision x_t from a feasible set X, then observes the loss function f_t(x) and the constraint function g_t(x) = [g_t^1(x), ..., g_t^N(x)]^T. Both may vary arbitrarily with time and are unknown before the decision is made.

P: min_{x_t in X} sum_{t=1}^T f_t(x_t) s.t. g_t(x_t) <= 0, for all t.

When g_t(x) = g(x) for all t, the problem reduces to the fixed-constraint setting.

3.1 Assumptions

  • Assumption 1. X is convex and bounded: there exists R > 0 such that ||x - y|| <= R for all x, y in X.
  • Assumption 2. The loss functions are convex with bounded subgradients: there exists D > 0 such that ||grad f_t(x)|| <= D for all x in X and all t.
  • Assumption 3. The constraint functions are convex and bounded: there exists G > 0 such that |g_t^n(x)| <= G for all x in X, all t, and all n.

The paper explicitly does not require the Slater condition. This is important because equality constraints, which are common in practice, are excluded by the usual shared interior-point assumption.

3.2 Performance Metrics

The paper evaluates constrained online learning against both offline and online solution benchmarks.

Static benchmark: x^* in arg min_{x in X} { sum_{t=1}^T f_t(x) | g_t(x) <= 0, for all t } Static regret: REG_s(T) = sum_{t=1}^T [ f_t(x_t) - f_t(x^*) ]
Dynamic benchmark: x_t^* in arg min_{x in X} { f_t(x) | g_t(x) <= 0, for all t } Dynamic regret: REG_d(T) = sum_{t=1}^T [ f_t(x_t) - f_t(x_t^*) ]
Soft constraint violation: VIO_s(T) = sum_{n=1}^N [ sum_{t=1}^T g_t^n(x_t) ]_+ Hard constraint violation: VIO_h(T) = sum_{n=1}^N sum_{t=1}^T [ g_t^n(x_t) ]_+

Soft violation allows compensation across time, while hard violation does not. Since VIO_s(T) <= VIO_h(T), a hard-violation bound also implies a soft-violation bound.

3.3 Variation Measures

The paper tracks dynamics through two variation measures.

Path length of the online solution benchmark: sum_{t=2}^T ||x_t^* - x_(t-1)^*|| = O(T^Vx)
Constraint variation: sum_{t=2}^T max_{x in X} ||g_t(x) - g_(t-1)(x)|| = O(T^Vg)

Here Vx and Vg lie in [0, 1]. When the benchmark is fixed, Vx = 0. When the constraints are fixed, Vg = 0.

4. Constrained Online Learning with Doubly-Bounded Queue (COLDQ)

4.1 Doubly-Bounded Virtual Queue

COLDQ introduces a virtual queue Q_t^n to track the violation of each time-varying constraint. After observing the constraint at the end of round t, the queue is updated by

Q_t^n = max { (1 - eta) Q_(t-1)^n + [ g_t^n(x_t) ]_+, gamma }

The extra term -(eta) Q_(t-1)^n keeps the queue from growing excessively, while the lower bound gamma prevents the queue from becoming too small. This leads to both a lower and an upper queue bound.

Lemma 1: gamma <= Q_t^n <= G / eta

4.2 Lyapunov Drift

The paper defines the following Lyapunov drift for t > 1:

Delta_(t-1) = (1 / 2) sum_{n=1}^N (Q_t^n - gamma)^2 - (1 / 2) sum_{n=1}^N (Q_(t-1)^n - gamma)^2

Unlike standard queue-based analyses, the Lyapunov function is centered around the lower bound gamma. This allows the drift bound to exploit both queue bounds at once.

Lemma 2: Delta_(t-1) <= sum_{n=1}^N Q_(t-1)^n [ g_(t-1)^n(x_t) ]_+ - gamma sum_{n=1}^N [ g_t^n(x_t) ]_+ + (G sqrt(N) / eta) max_{x in X} ||g_t(x) - g_(t-1)(x)|| + 2 N G^2

4.3 Per-Slot Optimization and Intuition

At each round t > 1, the learner chooses x_t by solving a one-step optimization problem that balances gradient descent on the previous loss and control of the previous constraint violations.

P_t: min_{x in X} < grad f_(t-1)(x_(t-1)), x - x_(t-1) > + alpha_(t-1) ||x - x_(t-1)||^2 + sum_{n=1}^N Q_(t-1)^n [ g_(t-1)^n(x) ]_+

If the gradient step does not violate the constraints, the optimizer follows the usual projected update. If it does, the queue-weighted constraint term shifts the direction to suppress violation. In this way, Q_(t-1)^n balances loss minimization and feasibility control.

4.4 Algorithm 1: COLDQ

  1. Initialize a non-decreasing sequence {alpha_t}, choose eta in (0, 1), gamma in (0, G / eta), pick any x_1 in X, and set Q_1^n = gamma for all n.
  2. For each t = 2, ..., T, update x_t by solving P_t.
  3. Observe grad f_t(x_t) and g_t(x).
  4. Update each queue Q_t^n using the doubly-bounded rule above.

5. Performance Bounds of COLDQ

Lemma 3: Per-Slot Performance

The analysis first derives a one-step inequality that upper-bounds the previous-step loss gap together with the queue-weighted constraint term. This becomes the starting point for both the dynamic regret and hard violation analysis.

For any t > 1: [ f_(t-1)(x_(t-1)) - f_(t-1)(x_(t-1)^*) ] + sum_{n=1}^N Q_(t-1)^n [ g_(t-1)^n(x_t) ]_+ <= 2 R alpha_(t-1) ||x_t^* - x_(t-1)^*|| + R^2 (alpha_t - alpha_(t-1)) + D^2 / (4 alpha_(t-1)) + ( alpha_(t-1) ||x_(t-1)^* - x_(t-1)||^2 - alpha_t ||x_t^* - x_t||^2 )

Theorem 1: Dynamic Regret

REG_d(T) <= 2 R sum_{t=2}^T alpha_(t-1) ||x_t^* - x_(t-1)^*|| + (D^2 / 4) sum_{t=1}^T (1 / alpha_t) + R^2 alpha_T + D R

Theorem 2: Hard Constraint Violation

VIO_h(T) <= (G sqrt(N) / (eta gamma)) sum_{t=2}^T max_{x in X} ||g_t(x) - g_(t-1)(x)|| + (2 R / gamma) sum_{t=2}^T alpha_(t-1) ||x_t^* - x_(t-1)^*|| + (D^2 / (4 gamma)) sum_{t=1}^T (1 / alpha_t) + ((D R + 2 N G^2) T) / gamma + (R^2 alpha_T) / gamma + N G

5.4 Strongly Convex Case

Under the additional assumption that each loss function is mu-strongly convex on X, the paper derives a tighter static regret guarantee.

Assumption 4: f_t(y) >= f_t(x) + < grad f_t(x), y - x > + mu ||y - x||^2, for all x, y in X and all t.
Theorem 3: REG_s(T) <= sum_{t=2}^{T-1} (alpha_t - alpha_(t-1) - mu) ||x^* - x_t||^2 + (D^2 / 4) sum_{t=1}^T (1 / alpha_t) + (alpha_1 - mu) R^2 + D R

5.5 Corollaries and Final Rates

Corollary 1 (Convex Loss): If alpha_t = t^((1 - Vx) / 2), eta = T^(-1), and gamma = epsilon T for epsilon in (0, G), then REG_d(T) = O(T^((1 + Vx) / 2)), VIO_h(T) = O(T^Vg)
Corollary 2 (Strongly Convex Loss): If alpha_t = mu t, eta = T^(-1), and gamma = epsilon T for epsilon in (0, G), then REG_s(T) = O(log T), VIO_h(T) = O(T^Vg)

Two important consequences follow immediately. First, by setting Vx = 0, the static regret reduces to O(T^(1/2)). Second, when constraints are fixed so that Vg = 0, the hard constraint violation becomes O(1). This is exactly the "keeping pace" behavior emphasized in the title of the paper.

Expert Tracking Extension

The paper further notes that the same O(T^((1 + Vx) / 2)) dynamic regret and O(T^Vg) hard constraint violation can be achieved without prior knowledge of Vx. The expert-tracking version runs multiple candidate schedules for alpha_t in parallel and aggregates them, preserving the asymptotic performance while removing the need to know the dynamics beforehand.

6. Experiments

The experiments report averages over 100 runs and evaluate COLDQ under both time-varying and fixed constraints, plus an application to online job scheduling.

6.1 Experiment on Time-Varying Constraints

Following Guo et al. (2022) and Yi et al. (2023), the paper sets the loss to f_t(x) = (1 / 2) ||H_t x - y_t||^2 with H_t in R^(4 x 10), x in R^10, and y_t in R^4. Each element of H_t is sampled uniformly from U(-1, 1). The constraints are g_t(x) = A_t x - b_t with A_t in R^(2 x 10), b_t in R^2, and feasible set X = {x | 0 <= x <= 5}.

The comparison is against RECOO and Algorithm 1 from Yi et al. (2023). The paper reports that COLDQ achieves more than 40% lower hard constraint violation than RECOO without sacrificing accumulated loss.

6.2 Experiment on Fixed Constraints

The fixed-constraint study uses an online quadratic programming problem similar to Yi et al. (2021), with f_t(x) = ||x - theta_t||^2 + 20 <theta_t, x>, where theta_t = theta_t^1 + theta_t^2 + theta_t^3 in R^2. It also evaluates an online linear programming variant with f_t(x) = <theta_t, x>. The fixed constraint is g(x) = A x - b with X = {x | 0 <= x <= 1}.

Against Yi et al. (2021) and RECOO, COLDQ again shows a significant reduction in hard constraint violation on both online quadratic programming and online linear programming.

6.3 Application to Online Job Scheduling

The paper applies COLDQ to online job scheduling using real-world datasets, following the setup of Yu, Neely, and Wei (2017) and Guo et al. (2022). The main reported metrics are time-averaged energy cost and average delayed jobs.

According to the paper, COLDQ significantly reduces the energy cost without compromising service quality when compared with RECOO and Algorithm 1 from Yi et al. (2023).

7. Conclusions

The paper proposes COLDQ, a constrained online learning algorithm for time-varying constraints that uses a virtual queue bounded from both above and below. A new Lyapunov drift analysis yields O(T^((1 + Vx) / 2)) dynamic regret and O(T^Vg) hard constraint violation. As the dynamics of the losses and constraints diminish, these guarantees smoothly approach the best-known O(T^(1/2)) regret and O(1) violation. For strongly convex losses, COLDQ matches the best-known O(log T) static regret while maintaining the same O(T^Vg) hard violation bound. The paper also presents an expert-tracking extension that removes the need for prior knowledge of the system dynamics and confirms the method empirically on multiple benchmarks.
